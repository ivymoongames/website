The design problem

How open can a guided adventure feel?

Project Mayhem uses broad exploratory spaces, points of interest, and recognizable landmarks between more controlled story transitions. The challenge was giving players room to wander without losing the thread of where to go next. The prototype combines wilderness navigation, optional discoveries, gated destinations, and a later shift into constructed mountain spaces.

02 / What the project demonstrates

Design decisions over scenery

For the Sunblink role, I'd keep the focus on how the world plays, not just its visual variety.

01 / WORLD ORGANIZATION

Open space, directed progression

The broad wilderness creates room for discovery while destinations like Blackthorn establish deliberate progression beats. The case study should make those relationships easy to read.

02 / OPTIONAL CONTENT

A reason to leave the path

Shrines, resources, and the Hel's Praise Shop create secondary goals. Their value comes from where players notice them, what draws them over, and what they receive.

03 / ENVIRONMENTAL VARIETY

Natural to constructed

Snowfields, lakeside ruins, guarded architecture, and the mountain interior provide distinct gameplay contexts. The lava lift is especially useful for demonstrating vertical spatial thinking.

04 / Next design pass

What I'd still validate

Before this becomes the public-facing case study, we should replace the approximate route drawing with actual level geometry and verify the design intent together.

Make it a real level-design document

Once UE is running, a top-down ortho capture lets us keep the center-out composition while being accurate about the playable space. Then we can add sightlines, gating rules, shortcuts, and reward placement where they're actually implemented.

  • True overhead layout and scale
  • Which detours reconnect, dead-end, or unlock
  • Gate requirements and intended player order
  • Specific traversal mechanics and encounter setups
  • Screenshot callouts tied to locations
